You are given a case directory containing a sparse downwind monitoring record for a plant with grouped emission sources. Your job is to recover the emission rate of each source cohort and predict the ground-level concentration field under a withheld meteorology that the monitors never saw, writing a single self-contained program /app/solve.py that reads one case directory and writes its results.

Inputs are provided under /app/data: example cases under /app/data/examples/ with their signed-off expected outputs, and supporting documents under /app/data/docs (field dictionary, dispersion specification with the sigma table, and the prior/covariance notes). Each graded case directory contains network.npz (source and receptor coordinates, cohort map, stack and exit-velocity data, monitor indices), meteorology.npz (calibration meteorological snapshots plus the withheld episode specification), observations.npz (monitor readings), noise_factor.npz (the noise covariance and its factor), and prior.json (a log-emission prior per cohort). One calibration snapshot has an incorrect stability class. The withheld episode lies in a conditions regime not represented in the calibration set.

The forward model follows the dispersion specification: a stability-dependent Gaussian plume with emission-dependent plume rise; recover the cohort emission rates from the monitoring record and forward-simulate the withheld meteorology to all receptors. Use the supplied prior and noise characterisation appropriately and assess calibration quality and internal consistency.

Write /app/output/reconstruction.npz with emission_coefficients (K,), emission_per_source (S,) consistent with the cohort map, and predicted_concentrations (R,) at the withheld meteorology, and /app/output/diagnostics.json with weighted_calibration_misfit, max_residual (maximum over receptors of the absolute difference between your prediction and an independent re-solution from your coefficients), max_predicted_concentration, and limit_exceeded (a boolean compliance determination against the stated short-term exposure limit). The program must finish within 120 seconds per case using only the provided python3 and standard library plus numpy and scipy; /app is read-only at runtime and the output path is the only writable location.

Grading uses unseen cases; reproduce the underlying behaviour rather than fit the examples. You have 28800 seconds to complete this task. Do not cheat by using online solutions or hints specific to this task.
